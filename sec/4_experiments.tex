\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\mysection{Models and domains.} We evaluate on Qwen2-7B-Instruct~\citep{yang2024qwen2} and LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct~\citep{dubey2024llama} across 22 datasets spanning QA, commonsense reasoning, math, knowledge, classification, code, and instruction-following (Appendix~\ref{app:domains}). For each dataset, we train an independent LoRA adapter (rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$) and merge it into a separate copy of the base model, yielding one fine-tuned checkpoint per dataset; adapters are never merged with one another.

\mysection{Safety benchmarks and judges.}
\textbf{BeaverTails}~\citep{ji2024beavertails} (700 evaluation prompts, disjoint from the split that supplies \ssn{} training data), \textbf{AdvBench}~\citep{zou2023universal} (520), and \textbf{HEx-PHI}~\citep{qi2024finetuning} (330) measure harmful rates; the latter two are never used in training. The primary judge is beaver-dam-7b~\citep{ji2024beavertails}; since it was trained on BeaverTails data, we re-score all LLaMA-3-8B outputs with the independently developed WildGuard~\citep{han2024wildguard}, whose documentation does not list BeaverTails among its training sources. We measure over-refusal on XSTest~\citep{rottger2024xstest} and OR-Bench~\citep{cui2025orbench}, and robustness to prompt transformations on JailbreakBench~\citep{chao2024jailbreakbench}, WildJailbreak~\citep{jiang2024wildteaming}, and DAN-style role-play templates~\citep{shen2024dan}.

\mysection{Evaluation protocol.}
Each of three held-out splits (Appendix~\ref{app:splits}) removes four datasets from different task categories; the hypernetwork is trained on the other 18 and evaluated on the four unseen ones. We report \textbf{Task Accuracy} (official test split, or a fixed-seed 20\% holdout when none exists) and \textbf{Harmful Rate} (\% of harmful prompts yielding harmful output). We compare against the aligned \textbf{base model}, the undefended \textbf{fine-tuned model}, and OneShot~\citep{zhang2026safety} on all 12 held-out checkpoints. On Split~A of LLaMA-3-8B, we additionally compare with Antidote~\citep{huang2025antidote} (Wanda-score pruning, $\alpha{=}0.2$), linear merging~\citep{farn2025safeguard} (equal interpolation of aligned and fine-tuned weights, $\lambda{=}0.5$), SafeLoRA~\citep{hsu2024safe}, and the guard models Llama Guard 3/4~\citep{dubey2024llama,meta2025llama4} and WildGuard.

\subsection{Main Results}

\input{tables/main_results}

Fine-tuning raises the average harmful rate from under 5\% (base) to 19--31\% (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}), most on instruction-following and commonsense domains. \method{} reduces the harmful rate to below 1\% on all 12 held-out datasets and both architectures, while losing less than 1\% task accuracy on average. OneShot lowers average accuracy by 15.3 (LLaMA-3-8B) and 6.4 (Qwen2-7B) points, by up to 68 points on one checkpoint (ARC), and leaves up to 14.7\% harmful responses.

\subsection{Is the Hypernetwork Necessary?}
\label{sec:shared}

Could a single \ssn{} replace the hypernetwork? For each architecture and split, we train one \emph{Shared \ssn{}} on all 18 training checkpoints with the same architecture, initialization, loss, data, and budget as the ground-truth \ssn{}s. It reads each target checkpoint's activations, but its weights are not generated from the fingerprint.

\input{tables/shared_ssn}

Table~\ref{tab:shared} shows that \method{} is better in all 24 architecture--split--metric comparisons (the closest is LLaMA-3-8B, Split~C, BeaverTails: 0.13 vs.\ 0.14). The Shared \ssn{} costs 3.3--4.7 accuracy points, consistent with a fixed decision boundary over-blocking some checkpoints, and still leaves up to 15.0\% harmful responses (Qwen2-7B, Split~B, HEx-PHI). Fingerprint-conditioned generation thus accounts for a substantial part of \method{}'s gains and held-out generalization. We attribute its transfer to unseen domains to the fact that fine-tuning on very different tasks degrades the same safety benchmarks (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}), consistent with safety relying on shared components~\citep{qi2024finetuning,wei2024assessing}: the fingerprint captures safety-relevant shifts rather than task identity. Our evidence covers the held-out domains tested here. Training an \ssn{} directly per checkpoint, which is how we obtain the supervision targets, costs 1.4 GPU-hours with safety data per checkpoint versus a 1.4\,s hypernetwork pass.

\subsection{Comparison with Post-hoc Defenses and Guard Models}
\label{sec:posthoc}

\input{tables/posthoc}

\mysection{Weight-modification baselines.} Antidote and linear merging keep accuracy close to the fine-tuned model (59.3 and 62.9 vs.\ 61.7 undefended; Table~\ref{tab:posthoc}) at no inference cost, but leave 7.8--9.3\% harmful responses on BeaverTails and 4.3--5.7\% on HEx-PHI, compared with at most 0.1\% for \method{}. Linear merging attains the highest accuracy, so the utility cost of weight modification depends on the method and operating point. Since each baseline is evaluated at one setting, these results characterize a favorable operating point for \method{} rather than a comparison of full trade-off curves.

\mysection{Over-refusal.} On XSTest and OR-Bench, \method{}'s benign-refusal rates are within 0.4 and 0.3 points of the aligned base model, while it refuses more genuinely harmful prompts (99.5\% and 99.0\% vs.\ 95.0\% and 97.0\%). Linear merging and Antidote refuse fewer benign but also fewer harmful prompts; SafeLoRA refuses only 80.8\% of OR-Bench toxic prompts (Appendix~\ref{app:posthoc}).

\mysection{Cost and pipeline complexity.} The pipeline has three standard supervised stages (LoRA fine-tuning, per-checkpoint \ssn{} training, and hypernetwork training), run once per backbone family by the model provider: 25.6 A100 GPU-hours for LLaMA-3-8B, excluding LoRA fine-tuning. Deploying on a new checkpoint needs only 50 calibration forward passes and one hypernetwork pass, with no gradients or tuning (Appendix~\ref{app:cost}). Loss coefficients are shared across architectures, splits, and ablations, and three hypernetwork seeds give accuracy s.d.\ $\leq$0.69 and harmful rates $\leq$0.14\% (Appendix~\ref{app:seeds}); we will release code and trained hypernetworks. At inference, the \ssn{} adds 3.4\% FLOPs and 10--15\,ms once per prompt (not per token), versus 175--244\% and 59--388\,ms for Llama Guard 3 and WildGuard; weight-modification methods add none.

\mysection{Guard models and judge dependence.} Llama Guard 4 (12B), used off the shelf and not trained on our data, misses 6.5\% of AdvBench and 6.7\% of HEx-PHI prompts, versus $\leq$0.05\% harmful for \method{} (Table~\ref{tab:llamaguard}). The two judges' split-level averages differ by $\leq$0.08 points and refusal rates by $\leq$1.7 (Appendix~\ref{app:judges}), and the judge is not saturated: it gives 19--31\% to undefended checkpoints.

\subsection{Robustness to Prompt Transformations}
\label{sec:robust}

Although trained only on directly phrased harmful prompts, \method{} detects 99.0\% of plain JailbreakBench requests and 92.2\% of WildJailbreak adversarial paraphrases, 40.6 points above Llama Guard 3 and 4.7 below the adversarially trained WildGuard (Table~\ref{tab:robust}). DAN-style role-play templates, which we pair with JailbreakBench behaviors, remain the most challenging setting (88.4\%), where both dedicated guards perform better.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}
\mysection{Loss components and training data} (LLaMA-3-8B, Split~A; Appendix~\ref{app:ablation_loss}). Removing $\mathcal{L}_\text{func}$ causes the classifier to over-block safe prompts, reducing accuracy across all domains; removing $\mathcal{L}_\text{cls}$ degrades both accuracy and safety; without domain-specific calibration prompts, the fingerprint is less discriminative and accuracy drops on harder domains. Excluding a whole task category from training (Appendix~\ref{app:ablation_data}) changes accuracy by at most 0.6 points, and removing QA or math/code data keeps the harmful rate $\leq$0.15\%; removing the six instruction-following datasets, however, raises it from 0.05\% to 4.88\%, suggesting that instruction-following checkpoints are the most important training data for safety.

\mysection{Calibration size and fine-tuning method.} Accuracy saturates at $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ with harmful rates $\leq$0.4\% throughout (Appendix~\ref{app:ncal}), and \ssn{}s generated for LoRA checkpoints transfer to fully fine-tuned ones (Appendix~\ref{app:fullft}).
