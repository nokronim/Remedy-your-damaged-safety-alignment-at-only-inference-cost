\mysection{SSN training data.} For each fine-tuned checkpoint, the ground-truth \ssn{} is trained on category-balanced harmful prompts from the BeaverTails training split ($\sim$7k prompts across 14 harm categories) together with in-domain safe prompts from that checkpoint's fine-tuning dataset. The harmful component is shared across checkpoints; the safe component is checkpoint-specific. The 50 calibration prompts used for the fingerprint are domain-specific and disjoint from the held-out probe set $\mathcal{P}_d$ used by $\mathcal{L}_\text{func}$.

\mysection{Loss coefficients.} The reconstruction, classification, and functional loss coefficients $(\beta_\text{recon},\beta_\text{cls},\beta_\text{func})=(1,10,5)$ were selected during development and then held fixed across both architectures, all three splits, and all ablations; they were not tuned per model or split. Reconstruction receives the lowest weight because matching the ground-truth weights does not by itself guarantee correct classification; classification receives the highest weight because it directly determines the answer/refuse decision; the functional loss receives an intermediate weight because it enforces agreement on held-out probe prompts. The values keep the three terms on comparable scales during training.

\section{Calibration Set Size}
\label{app:ncal}
We sweep the number of calibration prompts $N_\text{cal}$ used to extract the activation fingerprint (Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_ncal}, Table~\ref{tab:ablation_ncal}). With too few prompts ($N_\text{cal}{=}10$), the fingerprint is noisy and the generated \ssn{} over-blocks safe prompts; accuracy improves up to $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ and plateaus beyond that, uniformly across BoolQ, ARC, Math, and Alpaca, while the harmful rate stays $\leq 0.4\%$ across the entire sweep. We use $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ as the default.

\section{Training and Inference Cost}
\label{app:cost}
All stages were run on a single A100 80GB GPU. Meta-training is a one-time cost per backbone family, paid by the provider that trains the hypernetwork: LoRA fine-tuning of the 18 training-domain checkpoints, ground-truth \ssn{} training for each (3 epochs over $\sim$7k BeaverTails harmful prompts plus in-domain safe prompts), and hypernetwork training ($\sim$230M parameters, 100 epochs). Table~\ref{tab:cost} reports the measured cost for LLaMA-3-8B, excluding LoRA fine-tuning. Per released checkpoint, a practitioner runs $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ calibration prompts through the checkpoint, one hypernetwork forward pass, and loads the generated \ssn{}; end to end this takes under one minute and needs no gradients, safety data, or weight modification. At inference, the \ssn{} adds 3.41\%, 3.42\%, and 3.45\% FLOPs at 128, 256, and 512 prompt tokens (1.791 to 1.852 TFLOPs at 128 tokens). Latency is paid once per prompt at scoring time (Table~\ref{tab:latency}); safe prompts then decode with the unmodified backbone. Table~\ref{tab:overhead} compares the added inference overhead of all methods.
