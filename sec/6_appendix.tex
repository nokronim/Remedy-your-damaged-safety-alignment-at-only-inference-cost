\section{Additional Results: Calibration Set Size, Full Fine-Tuning Generalization, Cross-Benchmark Transfer}
\label{app:additional}

\paragraph{Sensitivity to calibration set size.}
Figure~\ref{fig:ablation_ncal} varies the number of calibration prompts $N_\text{cal}$ used to extract the activation fingerprint. With too few prompts ($N_\text{cal}{=}10$), the fingerprint is noisy: the generated \ssn{} produces safety scores skewed toward $\lambda{>}0.5$ even for safe prompts, causing over-blocking that degrades accuracy while achieving an artificially low harmful rate. This reflects fingerprint noise rather than threshold sensitivity --- at $N_\text{cal}{\ge}50$ the classifier separates safe and harmful prompts cleanly at the fixed threshold $\lambda{=}0.5$. Accuracy improves steadily up to $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ and plateaus beyond that. We use $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ as the default. Per-domain values are reported above in Table~\ref{tab:ablation_ncal}.

\begin{figure}[h]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.55\linewidth]{figures/ablation_ncal.pdf}
	\caption{Effect of calibration set size $N_\text{cal}$ (LLaMA-3, Split~A holdout). Avg.\ task accuracy saturates at $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ (our default) while the harmful rate stays near zero; larger $N_\text{cal}$ gives no further gains.}
	\label{fig:ablation_ncal}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Generalization to full fine-tuning.}
Our hypernetwork is trained on LoRA-merged models, but practitioners also use full-parameter fine-tuning. To test whether \method{} generalizes across fine-tuning methods, we fully fine-tune LLaMA-3-8B on four datasets and apply the same \ssn{}s generated for their LoRA counterparts. Table~\ref{tab:full_ft} shows that safety is restored equally well regardless of fine-tuning method: harmful rates drop to 0.0--0.1\% for both LoRA and full fine-tuning. Task accuracy deltas are comparable (within 0.3 percentage points), indicating that the activation fingerprint captures model-level safety patterns rather than LoRA-specific artifacts.

\paragraph{Cross-benchmark safety transfer.}
BeaverTails is the only harmful benchmark used during \ssn{} training (with a disjoint evaluation split). To test whether generated \ssn{}s generalize beyond BeaverTails, we evaluate on AdvBench (520 prompts) and HEx-PHI (330 prompts, 11 categories), which are never seen in any training phase. Per-domain harmful rates across all three benchmarks are reported in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}: AdvBench achieves 0\% harmful rate across the board, and HEx-PHI remains below 1\% on both architectures. This suggests that \method{} generalizes beyond benchmark-specific patterns.
