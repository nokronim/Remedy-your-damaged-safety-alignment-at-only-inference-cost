\begin{abstract}
Safety alignment in large language models is fragile under fine-tuning: even benign task adaptation can substantially increase harmful compliance. Current defenses either retrain each checkpoint individually---requiring safety data and per-model compute---or deploy model-agnostic classifiers that cannot account for how a particular fine-tuning recipe has reshaped the model's safety boundaries.

We propose \method{}, a framework that generates a lightweight, model-specific safety classifier for any fine-tuned checkpoint at inference time. \method{} treats intermediate activations of a fine-tuned model as a compact behavioral fingerprint and trains a hypernetwork to map these activations to the parameters of a side network that acts as a prompt-level safety classifier---a \textit{Safe Side Network} (\ssn{}). Two design choices make the high-dimensional weight prediction tractable: layer-wise factorized generation, which predicts each side-network layer independently, and direction-magnitude decomposition, which disentangles \textit{what} fine-tuning changed from \textit{how much}. At deployment, a single forward pass through the trained hypernetwork produces a complete \ssn{} from a small set of calibration prompts, with no gradient computation and no modification of the target model.

We evaluate across two model families (Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B), diverse fine-tuning datasets spanning QA, commonsense reasoning, math, and instruction-following, and three safety benchmarks. Generated \ssn{}s virtually eliminate harmful outputs while preserving downstream task accuracy, and transfer zero-shot to safety benchmarks never seen during training.
\end{abstract}
