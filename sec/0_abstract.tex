\begin{abstract}
Safety alignment in large language models (LLMs) is fragile under downstream fine-tuning: even benign adaptation can substantially weaken refusal behaviors and increase vulnerability to jailbreaks. Existing mitigation strategies often rely on additional alignment data, repeated post-hoc training, or model-specific guardrails, which do not scale to the growing ecosystem of fine-tuned checkpoints and adapters. We propose a model-conditioned safety framework that instantiates a lightweight, prompt-level safety head for each fine-tuned LLM at inference time, without retraining. Our key idea is to treat intermediate activations of a fine-tuned LLM as a compact behavioral signature and train a hypernetwork that maps these activations to the parameters of a small side network that predicts a safety score for an input prompt. To improve stability and identifiability, we factorize weight generation and predict the parameters of each side-network layer independently from corresponding LLM activations. Optionally, we augment activations with behavioral probe fingerprints to improve generalization across fine-tuning recipes and checkpoints. Experiments across diverse fine-tuning settings and unseen models show that the generated safety heads recover a significant portion of degraded safety behavior while preserving downstream task performance, enabling scalable inference-time safety evaluation and control.
\end{abstract}
