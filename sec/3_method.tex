\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Our proposed \method{} framework generates a model-specific safety head for recovering safety in fine-tuned language models. The safety head is a lightweight side network that performs prompt-level safety classification, and its weights are predicted by a hypernetwork from activation fingerprints of the target model. During training, we collect pairs of fine-tuned models and their corresponding safety heads across multiple domains to supervise the hypernetwork. At inference, \method{} produces a safety head for a fine-tuned model whose safety alignment may have degraded using only a small set of calibration prompts. In the following sections, we describe the problem setup, the architecture, and the hypernetwork training and deployment procedure.

% \method{} operates in three phases: (1)~collect ground-truth safety heads for a set of training domains (\S\ref{sec:gt}), (2)~train a hypernetwork to predict safety head weights from model activations (\S\ref{sec:hypernet}), and (3)~at inference, generate a safety head for any new fine-tuned model in one forward pass (\S\ref{sec:deploy}).

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathcal{M}_0$ be a safety-aligned base LLM and $\mathcal{M}_d$ a fine-tuned version of $\mathcal{M}_0$ on domain $d$. Fine-tuning may weaken or alter the safety behavior of $\mathcal{M}_d$, causing it to comply with harmful prompts that $\mathcal{M}_0$ would refuse. We seek a generator $G$ that, given any fine-tuned model $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$ from a potentially unseen domain, produces a safety head $f_\theta$:
\begin{equation}
\theta = G\!\big(\mathbf{A}(\mathcal{M}_{d^*})\big), \quad \lambda = f_\theta(x),
\end{equation}
where $x$ denotes the input prompt, $\mathbf{A}(\cdot)$ denotes activations extracted from a small calibration set, and $\lambda \in [0,1]$ is a safety score ($\lambda \approx 0$: safe; $\lambda \approx 1$: harmful). In our framework, the generator $G$ is implemented as a hypernetwork that predicts the parameters $\theta$ of the safety head from the activation fingerprint of the fine-tuned model (see Sec \ref{sec:hypernet}).

\subsection{\method{} Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

The safety head $f_\theta$ is a lightweight side network attached to the frozen backbone that predicts a safety score for each prompt and determines whether the model should produce a response or refuse. We build on the Ladder Side-Tuning (LST) architecture~\cite{sung2022lst} and extend it for safety classification. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} illustrates the overall design.

\mysection{Side Transformer.} The safety head is implemented as a small Transformer that processes ladder-fused representations derived from the backbone hidden states. It consists of $K{=}12$ layers with hidden dimension $h_s = h/r$. We set $r{=}4$, yielding $h_s{=}896$ with 7 attention heads for Qwen2-7B and $h_s{=}1024$ with 8 attention heads for LLaMA-3-8B (i.e., $\sim$3\% of backbone parameters).

\mysection{Ladder Connections.} Each side layer $k$ fuses downsampled backbone hidden states with side hidden states via a learnable gate:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}^{(k)}_\text{in} = \mu_k \cdot W_\text{down}\, \mathbf{h}^{(l)}_\text{back} + (1{-}\mu_k) \cdot \mathbf{h}^{(k-1)}_\text{side}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{h}^{(l)}_\text{back}$ denotes the backbone hidden state at layer $l$, $\mathbf{h}^{(k-1)}_\text{side}$ denotes the side network hidden state from the previous layer, and $\mu_k = \sigma(\alpha_k/\tau)$ is a learned gate.

\mysection{Selective Routing.} After the final side layer, hidden states are mean-pooled and passed through an MLP to produce $\lambda$. At inference, prompts scoring above the threshold are routed to a refusal response; those below bypass the side network entirely, so the base model runs unmodified. This selective routing preserves task accuracy because safe prompts never interact with the safety head.

\subsection{Activation-Conditioned Hypernetwork}
\label{sec:hypernet}

The safety head described above defines the classifier architecture. We now describe how its parameters are generated automatically from activation fingerprints. The hypernetwork $G$ takes the activation fingerprint of a fine-tuned model as input and predicts the parameters of the safety head. To make the high-dimensional weight prediction feasible, we employ two complementary design strategies.


\mysection{Activation Fingerprint Extraction. } For each model $\mathcal{M}_d$, we run $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ domain-specific calibration prompts and average the backbone hidden state at each layer $l$ after the final token:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a}_l^{(d)} = \frac{1}{N_\text{cal}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{cal}} \mathbf{h}_l^{(d)}(x_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{h}
\end{equation}
These $L$ vectors form a compact behavioral fingerprint of how fine-tuning shifted the model's representations.

\mysection{Direction-Magnitude Decomposition.} Each activation vector is split into a unit direction and log-magnitude:
% \begin{equation}
%     \hat{\mathbf{d}}_l = \frac{\mathbf{a}_l}{\|\mathbf{a}_l\|}, \quad m_l = \log \|\mathbf{a}_l\|
% \end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{d}}_l^{(d)} = \frac{\mathbf{a}_l^{(d)}}{\|\mathbf{a}_l^{(d)}\|}, \quad m_l^{(d)} = \log \|\mathbf{a}_l^{(d)}\|
\end{equation}
% The direction encodes what changed; the magnitude encodes how much. Separate encoders process each component:
The direction captures the nature of the representational change, while the magnitude reflects its strength.
% \begin{equation}
%     \mathbf{z}_\text{dir} = \text{Attn}(W_\text{proj}\,\hat{\mathbf{d}}_l), \quad
%     \mathbf{z}_\text{mag} = \text{MLP}(m_l)
% \end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_\text{dir} = \text{Attn}(W_\text{proj}\,\hat{\mathbf{d}}_l^{(d)}), \quad
    \mathbf{z}_\text{mag} = \text{MLP}(m_l^{(d)})
\end{equation}
The fused representation $[\mathbf{z}_\text{dir};\, \mathbf{z}_\text{mag}]$ feeds the weight-generation MLP.

\mysection{Layer-wise Factorized Generation.} Rather than predicting all parameters jointly, we generate each side layer's weights independently from the corresponding backbone activation:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k = H_k(\mathbf{a}_{l(k)}^{(d)}),
\end{equation}
where $l(k)$ denotes the backbone layer associated with side layer $k$. The modules $H_k$ share most parameters across layers but include layer-specific adapters, reducing the effective output dimensionality per prediction.

\mysection{Classifier Generation.} The safety head classifier is generated via a dedicated branch that aggregates information across all layers using cross-layer attention, producing a global embedding of the model's behavioral shift. This embedding is mapped through an MLP with a low-rank output factorization ($W_\text{cls} = B \cdot A$, rank $r{=}16$) for efficiency.

\subsection{Training and Deployment}
\label{sec:deploy}

% Training proceeds in two stages. First, we train domain-specific safety heads to obtain supervision for the hypernetwork. Second, the hypernetwork is trained to generate these weights from activation fingerprints.
Training proceeds in two stages. First, we train domain-specific safety heads for a set of fine-tuned models to obtain supervision pairs consisting of activation fingerprints and corresponding safety head weights. Second, the hypernetwork is trained to map activation fingerprints to these weights, enabling it to generate safety heads for new fine-tuned models at inference time.

\mysection{Ground-truth Safety Head Collection.} We collect supervised pairs $\{(\mathcal{M}_d, \theta_d^*)\}_{d=1}^K$ by training one side network per domain:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fine-tune $\mathcal{M}_0$ on domain $d$ with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$); merge the adapter to obtain $\mathcal{M}_d$.
    \item Freeze $\mathcal{M}_d$; attach a randomly initialized side network.
    \item Train the side network on a mixture of domain-specific safe data and category-balanced harmful data from BeaverTails~\cite{ji2024beavertails} ($\sim$7k samples).
\end{enumerate}
Training uses a selective loss: harmful prompts receive both language-modeling loss and classification loss; safe prompts receive only classification loss. This teaches the side network when to intervene without degrading task performance on safe inputs. All side networks share the same initialization, ensuring weight-space alignment for hypernetwork supervision.

\mysection{Hypernetwork Training.} The hypernetwork is trained on $K$ domains (with $M$ held out) using three complementary losses:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{recon} + \alpha\, \mathcal{L}_\text{cls} + \beta\, \mathcal{L}_\text{func}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{recon}$ is the MSE between generated and ground-truth side network weights, providing dense supervision across all parameters; $\mathcal{L}_\text{cls}$ is BCE on safety score predictions using the calibration prompts ($\alpha{=}10$); and $\mathcal{L}_\text{func}$ is BCE on a separate batch of safe and harmful probe prompts ($\beta{=}5$), verifying that the generated classifier generalizes beyond the calibration set. Training runs for 100 epochs with learning rate $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and hidden dimension 512.

\mysection{Deployment.} Given a new fine-tuned model $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$: (1)~extract layer-wise activations from $N_\text{cal}$ calibration prompts; (2)~run one forward pass through the hypernetwork to obtain safety head weights; (3)~load the safety head alongside $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$. The entire process completes in under one minute on a single GPU, with no safety data, no gradients, and no model modification.
