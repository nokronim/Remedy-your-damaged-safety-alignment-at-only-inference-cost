\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_works}

\paragraph{Safety Alignment of LLMs.}
Safety alignment is typically achieved via supervised fine-tuning on curated demonstration data~\cite{ouyang2022training, bai2022training}, reinforcement learning from human feedback~\cite{stiennon2020summarization, christiano2017deep}, constitutional AI~\cite{bai2022constitutional}, and direct preference optimization~\cite{rafailov2023dpo}. These procedures have produced production-grade aligned models such as Llama-3-Instruct~\cite{dubey2024llama}, Qwen2-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen2}, Mistral~\cite{jiang2023mistral}, and GPT-4~\cite{openai2023gpt4}. Despite their effectiveness, the resulting alignment is shallow: \citet{qi2025tokensdeep} show that refusal behavior is encoded in only a few token positions, and \citet{wei2024assessing} find that safety lives in low-rank subspaces that adversarial fine-tuning can easily traverse.

\paragraph{Safety Degradation under Fine-Tuning.}
\citet{qi2024finetuning} first demonstrated that fine-tuning aligned LLMs on benign data compromises safety, even without malicious intent. Follow-up work confirmed that the effect is robust across architectures~\cite{fraser2025finetuninglowers, lermen2024lora}, model scales~\cite{zhan2024removing}, and parameter-efficient methods~\cite{hu2022lora, dettmers2023qlora}. Adversarial fine-tuning amplifies the risk: a handful of harmful examples~\cite{qi2024finetuning} or even purely benign-looking gradient updates~\cite{he2024whats} can fully unlock a previously aligned model. Beyond fine-tuning, standard merging~\cite{ilharco2023task, yadav2023ties} and quantization~\cite{egashira2024quantizationattack} similarly degrade alignment.

\paragraph{Defenses at the Fine-Tuning Stage.}
A first line of defense modifies the fine-tuning process itself. LISA~\cite{huang2024lisa} alternates between task and alignment objectives, Vaccine~\cite{yi2024vaccine} adds perturbation-aware updates, RepNoise~\cite{rosati2024representation} injects representation-level noise, Constrained-SFT~\cite{qi2024safetypreservation} freezes shallow safety subspaces, and SafeInstr~\cite{bianchi2024safetytuned} augments the fine-tuning data with safety demonstrations. Booster~\cite{huang2024booster} combines safety probing with selective gradient masking. All of these methods produce a Pareto trade-off between downstream utility and residual harm, and require coordination with the entity performing the fine-tuning --- they cannot be retroactively applied to released checkpoints.

\paragraph{Post-Fine-Tuning Weight-Modification Defenses.}
A second line of defense modifies an already fine-tuned checkpoint. Safe LoRA~\cite{hsu2024safe} projects LoRA updates onto an alignment-preserving subspace estimated from $(\textsc{base}, \textsc{aligned})$ weight differences. OneShot SFT~\cite{zhang2026safety} performs full fine-tuning on a single safety example for a few epochs to restore refusal. Task-arithmetic methods~\cite{ilharco2023task, yadav2023ties} subtract estimated ``harm vectors'' from the fine-tuned weights. RESTA~\cite{bhardwaj2024resta} restores safety by adding a fixed safety vector. \citet{wang2024backdooralignment} and \citet{liu2024robustalign} explore representation-engineering interventions. These methods all alter the deployed weights, which can degrade task accuracy in unpredictable ways and require access to the original aligned model at deployment.

\paragraph{Model-Agnostic Safety Classifiers.}
Llama Guard~\cite{inan2023llama} and its successors (Llama Guard 2/3/4) classify prompts and responses against a fixed taxonomy. ShieldGemma~\cite{zeng2024shieldgemma}, WildGuard~\cite{han2024wildguard}, Aegis~\cite{ghosh2024aegis}, and Granite Guardian~\cite{padhi2024granite} extend coverage to additional harm categories and adversarial prompt patterns. \citet{markov2023moderation} and \citet{lees2022newgeneration} introduce production moderation classifiers. These guardrails are model-agnostic, which makes them broadly deployable but blind to recipe-specific failure modes in fine-tuned downstream models.

\paragraph{Internal-State Probing for Safety.}
A complementary line of work probes a model's own hidden states for harmfulness signals. HiddenDetect~\cite{jiang2025hiddendetect} monitors mid-layer activations to flag attacks; \citet{macdiarmid2024simpleprobes} train linear probes on residual-stream activations; \citet{azaria2023internal} detect untruthful responses via self-consistency. These methods exploit information that is only available inside the target model, but require fitting per-model probes that do not transfer to new fine-tuned checkpoints.

\paragraph{Jailbreak Attacks.}
A wide range of attacks circumvent safety alignment: gradient-based suffixes~\cite{zou2023universal, zou2023representation}, persona/roleplay prompts~\cite{wei2023jailbroken, shen2024donowanything}, multilingual~\cite{deng2024multilingual} and low-resource attacks~\cite{yong2023lowresource}, as well as fine-tuning-based attacks~\cite{qi2024finetuning, lermen2024lora}. Defenses against these attacks (e.g., SmoothLLM~\cite{robey2024smoothllm}, paraphrasing-based detection~\cite{jain2023baseline}) typically operate at inference time and are orthogonal to defenses against fine-tuning-induced erosion --- which is the focus of our work.

\paragraph{Hypernetworks and Side Networks.}
Hypernetworks~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks, chauhan2024brief} are neural networks that generate the weights of a target network conditioned on some context. They have been applied to continual learning~\cite{vonoswald2020continual}, multi-task adaptation~\cite{navon2023equivariant}, parameter-efficient fine-tuning~\cite{phang2023hypertuning, mahabadi2021parameterefficient}, and zero-shot model construction~\cite{knyazev2021graphhypernet}. Recently, \citet{liang2025dragdrop} scaled this paradigm to LLMs, generating LoRA parameters directly from task descriptions for zero-shot \emph{utility} adaptation. Our work pursues a complementary direction: rather than generating task-adaptation weights from text prompts, we generate \emph{safety} weights from model activations, targeting the orthogonal problem of alignment restoration. Ladder Side-Tuning (LST)~\cite{sung2022lst} introduces a lightweight side network connected to a frozen backbone via lateral connections for parameter-efficient transfer. We repurpose this architecture as a safety classifier and are, to our knowledge, the first to combine HyperNetworks with side-network architectures specifically for safety restoration in fine-tuned LLMs.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
We evaluate using BeaverTails~\cite{ji2024beavertails} (700 prompts across 14 harm categories), AdvBench~\cite{zou2023universal} (520 adversarial behaviors), and HEx-PHI~\cite{qi2024hexphi} (300 prompts across 11 OpenAI policy categories). Refusal correctness is judged by \texttt{beaver-dam-7b}~\cite{ji2024beavertails}, the standard moderation classifier in the safety literature.
