\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_works}

\paragraph{Safety degradation under fine-tuning.}
LLMs are aligned with supervised fine-tuning and RLHF~\citep{christiano2017deep, stiennon2020summarization, ouyang2022training, bai2022training}, Constitutional AI~\citep{bai2022constitutional}, or DPO~\citep{rafailov2023dpo}. This alignment is shallow: refusal is concentrated in the first few output tokens~\citep{qi2025safety} and in sparse, low-rank weight regions~\citep{wei2024assessing}. \citet{qi2024finetuning} showed that fine-tuning, even on benign data, compromises safety~\citep[see also][]{fraser2025finetuninglowers}, and fine-tuning on harmful data removes safety training efficiently even for 70B and proprietary models~\citep{lermen2023lora, zhan2024removing}; narrow fine-tuning can also produce broad misalignment~\citep{betley2025emergent}.

\paragraph{Defenses against fine-tuning-induced degradation.}
Following the stage-based taxonomy of \citet{huang2024survey}, we group defenses by when they act. \emph{(i) Alignment- and fine-tuning-stage defenses} shape training so that later fine-tuning does less damage: perturbation-aware alignment (Vaccine~\citep{huang2024vaccine}, Booster~\citep{huang2024booster}), representation noising~\citep{rosati2024representation}, security vectors~\citep{zhou2024making}, and constrained or safety-augmented fine-tuning (Lisa~\citep{huang2024lisa}, SafeInstr~\citep{bianchi2024safetytuned}, BackdoorAlign~\citep{wang2024backdooralign}, token-constrained SFT~\citep{qi2025safety}). They assume control over training and do not apply to checkpoints that are already released. \emph{(ii) Post-fine-tuning weight repair} edits a released checkpoint: projection onto a safety subspace (Safe LoRA~\citep{hsu2024safe}), task-vector arithmetic and merging (Task Arithmetic~\citep{ilharco2023task}, TIES~\citep{yadav2023ties}, RESTA~\citep{bhardwaj2024resta}, linear merging~\citep{farn2025safeguard}), pruning of harmful parameters (Antidote~\citep{huang2025antidote}), post-hoc perturbation (Panacea~\citep{wang2025panacea}), and one-shot re-alignment (OneShot~\citep{zhang2026safety}). These methods add no inference cost, but change the deployed weights, expose a coefficient that sets the safety--utility balance, and in some cases need the aligned base model. \emph{(iii) Guard models} filter inputs or outputs without inspecting the target model (Llama Guard~\citep{inan2023llama}, ShieldGemma~\citep{zeng2024shieldgemma}, WildGuard~\citep{han2024wildguard}, AEGIS~\citep{ghosh2024aegis}, Granite Guardian~\citep{padhi2024granite}, and production moderation systems~\citep{markov2023moderation, lees2022newgeneration}); a related line probes a model's own hidden states~\citep{azaria2023internal, zou2023representation, arditi2024refusal, jiang2025hiddendetect}. \method{} combines properties of (ii) and (iii): like weight repair it is specific to the target checkpoint, and like guard models it leaves the deployed weights unchanged. Unlike hidden-state probes, which are trained per model, its classifier is generated for a new checkpoint without training.

\paragraph{Hypernetworks and side networks.}
Hypernetworks~\citep{ha2017hypernetworks, chauhan2024brief} generate the weights of a target network conditioned on context, with applications in continual learning~\citep{vonoswald2020continual}, weight-space learning~\citep{navon2023equivariant}, parameter-efficient fine-tuning~\citep{phang2023hypertuning, mahabadi2021parameterefficient}, and zero-shot model construction~\citep{knyazev2021graphhypernet}. \citet{liang2025dragdrop} scaled the paradigm to LLMs, generating LoRA parameters from text prompts for zero-shot \emph{utility} adaptation. \method{} instead generates weights of a Ladder Side-Tuning network~\citep{sung2022lst} from \textit{model activations}, and to our knowledge is the first to combine hypernetworks with side networks for safety restoration.
