\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fine-tuning a safety-aligned LLM on a downstream task is the standard way to specialize today's open-weight models~\cite{ouyang2022training, bai2022training, bai2022constitutional} --- and it routinely breaks the safety alignment of the base model. Even when the fine-tuning data contains no harmful content, the harmful-response rate of a LoRA-fine-tuned Instruct model can climb from under 5\% (the aligned baseline) to over 40\% on standard safety benchmarks~\cite{qi2024finetuning, fraser2025finetuninglowers, lermen2024lora, zhan2024removing}. The cause is structural: refusal behavior is encoded in only a handful of shallow token-position subspaces~\cite{qi2025tokensdeep, wei2024assessing}, so even task-relevant gradient updates on benign data can shift the model outside the aligned region within tens of steps.

The scale of the problem is set by the deployment ecosystem. Hugging Face hosts hundreds of thousands of LoRA adapters~\cite{hu2022lora} and merged checkpoints, none of which carry a guarantee that their base model's safety survives the fine-tuning recipe. Restoring safety on each checkpoint individually requires a per-model intervention; defending all of them with a single off-the-shelf classifier ignores how each fine-tuning recipe has reshaped its target. Existing defenses pick one of these two horns, and pay the corresponding price.

\paragraph{Why existing remedies fall short.}
Defenses against fine-tuning-induced safety erosion fall into three families, each with a fundamental limitation:

\textit{(i) Fine-tuning-stage methods} modify the optimization process itself --- e.g., LISA's lazy alignment~\cite{huang2024lisa}, Vaccine's perturbation-aware updates~\cite{yi2024vaccine}, RepNoise~\cite{rosati2024representation}, SafeInstr's safety-augmented data~\cite{bianchi2024safetytuned}, and Constrained-SFT~\cite{qi2024safetypreservation}. These methods treat safety preservation as an additional optimization constraint, which inherently produces a trade-off between downstream task performance and residual harm: tighter safety regularization always sacrifices accuracy on the target task, and looser regularization leaks harmful content. They also require coordination with the fine-tuner --- they cannot be retroactively applied to checkpoints that have already been released.

\textit{(ii) Post-fine-tuning weight-modification methods} project or merge the fine-tuned weights to recover alignment --- e.g., Safe LoRA's projection onto an alignment subspace~\cite{hsu2024safe}, OneShot SFT~\cite{zhang2026safety} that performs full fine-tuning on a single safety example, and TaskArithmetic~\cite{ilharco2023task} for safety-task vector subtraction. These approaches modify the deployed weights, which (a) can degrade task accuracy in unpredictable ways, (b) require access to the original aligned base model at deployment time, and (c) operate on the same fundamental trade-off curve as fine-tuning-stage methods.

\textit{(iii) Model-agnostic external classifiers} sit in front of the LLM and filter inputs/outputs --- e.g., Llama Guard~\cite{inan2023llama}, ShieldGemma~\cite{zeng2024shieldgemma}, WildGuard~\cite{han2024wildguard}, Aegis~\cite{ghosh2024aegis}, and Granite Guardian~\cite{padhi2024granite}. Because they ignore the specific fine-tuning recipe, they miss recipe-specific failure modes (e.g., jailbreaks that align with newly learned domain syntax) and waste capacity on out-of-distribution risks the deployed model never faces.

A defender therefore has to choose between paying a per-checkpoint training cost (families i and ii) or accepting recipe-blind protection (family iii). Neither is satisfactory at the scale of today's adapter ecosystem.

\paragraph{Our research question.}
We ask:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Is it possible to train a generalizable hypernetwork that, given any fine-tuned checkpoint, efficiently produces a lightweight side network restoring the safety behavior of the original aligned model --- without retraining, without modifying the deployed weights, and without sacrificing downstream accuracy?}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Our motivation.}
Fine-tuning-stage and weight-modification methods sit on a single Pareto frontier: utility on one axis, safety on the other. Our key insight is that this frontier exists only because the safety mechanism is forced to share parameters with the task mechanism. If safety is instead delegated to a separate, conditionally engaged \textit{side network}~\cite{sung2022lst} that runs in parallel with the frozen fine-tuned model, then (a) the fine-tuned model retains its full task capacity (utility from the fine-tuned model), and (b) the side network can be trained to perfectly restore refusal on harmful prompts (safety from a pre-trained safety model). We thus get an ensemble that picks up the utility of the fine-tuned model and the safety of the original aligned model --- escaping the trade-off rather than navigating it.

The remaining problem is cost: training a bespoke side network for every fine-tuned checkpoint is impractical at the scale of the adapter ecosystem. We address this by training a \textit{hypernetwork}~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks, chauhan2024brief} that, given a small set of activation fingerprints from a new fine-tuned model, generates the side network's weights in a single forward pass --- no gradient updates, no safety data needed at deployment time. The hypernetwork is trained once and amortizes across an unbounded number of downstream checkpoints.

\paragraph{\method{}.}
Concretely, we introduce \method{}, which combines three innovations to make this generation tractable:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textit{Activation-conditioned weight generation}: the safety side network is produced from a per-prompt fingerprint of how the frozen fine-tuned model's intermediate activations change under fine-tuning, with no gradient computation at deployment time.
    \item \textit{Local + global hypernetwork conditioning}: each side-network layer's weights are generated from both the corresponding base layer's local activation (depth-specific specialization) and a global mean across selected base layers (cross-layer coherence), enabling per-prompt specialization with stable cross-layer composition.
    \item \textit{Lightweight side network with hard-threshold routing}: at inference, a $\lambda$-classifier predicts a routing probability per prompt; if $\lambda < 0.5$ the base model runs as-is, and if $\lambda \ge 0.5$ a fixed refusal template is substituted, eliminating decode-time overhead on safe prompts and yielding a net speed-up on harmful ones.
\end{enumerate}
The side network adds only $\sim$3\% parameters over the base model, with no decode-time overhead on the typical benign-prompt path. The hypernetwork is base-model-agnostic and generalizes across fine-tuning recipes (12 unseen domains $\times$ 2 architectures), demonstrating that a single trained hypernetwork can be deployed against new checkpoints zero-shot. \paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
    \item We formulate safety restoration as \textit{activation-conditioned generation}: the side network is automatically produced from each fine-tuned model's activation fingerprint, requiring neither per-model training nor safety data at deployment.
    \item We design \method{}, combining layer-wise factorized weight generation with local+global activation conditioning to make high-dimensional weight prediction stable and generalizable.
    \item We provide an inference-cost analysis showing that the side network adds only $\sim$3\% parameters over the base model, with zero decode-time overhead on safe prompts and a net speed-up on refused ones.
    \item We validate on two architectures (Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B): the hypernetwork is trained on 22 fine-tuning datasets and evaluated zero-shot on unseen fine-tuned checkpoints from three holdout splits of 4 held-out datasets each (12 held-out evaluations total), using three safety benchmarks (BeaverTails plus two fully zero-shot: AdvBench and HEx-PHI). \method{} achieves near-complete harmful-prompt elimination with $<$1pp accuracy loss vs.\ the unmodified fine-tuned model.
\end{enumerate}
