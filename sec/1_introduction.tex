\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models ship with safety alignment that curbs harmful outputs~\cite{ouyang2022training, bai2022training}. Yet this alignment is brittle: downstream fine-tuning---even on purely benign data---can erode refusal behavior in as few as a hundred steps~\cite{qi2024finetuning, huang2024lisa, fraser2025finetuninglowers}. The open-weight ecosystem amplifies the risk: thousands of community-contributed LoRA adapters and merged checkpoints circulate without any guarantee that the original safety properties survive. Existing defenses either retrain each checkpoint individually~\cite{huang2024lisa, hsu2024safe, yi2024vaccine}, incurring per-model cost, or deploy model-agnostic classifiers~\cite{inan2023llama, han2024wildguard} that cannot account for how a specific fine-tuning recipe has shifted safety boundaries.

We propose a different approach: since each fine-tuning recipe shifts internal representations in a distinct way, we use these shifts as a fingerprint to \textit{generate} a model-specific safety classifier automatically.

The key insight is that fine-tuning leaves a characteristic signature in the model's intermediate representations. Even when two models are fine-tuned on unrelated tasks, the pattern of activation shifts across layers encodes information about how safety-relevant features have been altered. By training a hypernetwork to read these signatures, we can predict what safety classifier each model needs without ever seeing harmful data at deployment time.

Concretely, we introduce \method{}, a \textit{hypernetwork}~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks} that takes intermediate activations from any fine-tuned model as input and outputs the weights of a \textit{Safe Side Network} (\ssn{})---a side network~\cite{sung2022lst} that classifies each prompt as safe or harmful. At deployment, a single forward pass through the hypernetwork produces a model-specific \ssn{} from only 50 calibration prompts, with no gradient computation and no model modification.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce activation-conditioned safety generation: the safety classifier is automatically produced from each fine-tuned model's activation fingerprint, requiring no per-model training or safety data at deployment.
    \item We propose \method{}, combining layer-wise factorized weight generation with direction-magnitude activation decomposition to make high-dimensional weight prediction tractable.
    \item We validate on two architectures (Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B) across 22 fine-tuning datasets with three holdout splits (4 held-out datasets each), and three safety benchmarks (two zero-shot), demonstrating near-complete harmful elimination with negligible accuracy loss.
\end{enumerate}
