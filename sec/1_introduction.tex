\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fine-tuning a safety-aligned pre-trained LLM on a downstream task is the standard way to specialize open-source models, but it routinely degrades the safety alignment of the base model. Even when the fine-tuning data contains no harmful content, the harmful-response rate of a LoRA fine-tuned model can climb from under 5\% (the aligned baseline) to over 40\% on standard safety benchmarks~\cite{qi2024finetuning}, because refusal behavior is encoded in only a handful of shallow token-position subspaces~\cite{qi2025safety}, so even task-relevant gradient updates on benign data can shift the model outside the aligned region within tens of steps.

The scale of the problem is set by the deployment ecosystem: a large and rapidly growing population of fine-tuned checkpoints, each derived from the same handful of safety-aligned base models, but none of which carries a guarantee that those safety properties survive the fine-tuning recipe. Restoring safety on each checkpoint individually requires a per-model intervention; defending all of them with a single off-the-shelf classifier ignores recipe-specific shifts in the target model.

\paragraph{Why existing remedies fall short.}
Defenses against fine-tuning-induced safety erosion fall into three families, each with a fundamental limitation:

\textbf{(i) Fine-tuning-stage methods} (LISA~\cite{huang2024lisa}, Vaccine~\cite{yi2024vaccine}, RepNoise~\cite{rosati2024representation}, SafeInstr~\cite{bianchi2024safetytuned}, Constrained-SFT~\cite{qi2025safety}) treat safety preservation as an additional optimization constraint during fine-tuning. This \textit{forces a Pareto trade-off between downstream utility and residual harm}: tighter safety regularization sacrifices accuracy on the target task, looser regularization leaks harmful content. They also require coordination with the fine-tuner --- they cannot be retroactively applied to checkpoints that have already been released.

\textbf{(ii) Post-fine-tuning weight-modification methods} project or merge the fine-tuned weights to recover alignment (Safe LoRA~\cite{hsu2024safe}, OneShot SFT~\cite{zhang2026safety}, TaskArithmetic~\cite{ilharco2023task}). These approaches \textit{modify the deployed weights}, which (a)~can degrade task accuracy in unpredictable ways, (b)~require access to the original aligned base model at deployment time, and (c)~operate on the same Pareto curve as fine-tuning-stage methods.

\textbf{(iii) Model-agnostic external classifiers} sit in front of the LLM and filter inputs/outputs (Llama Guard~\cite{inan2023llama}, ShieldGemma~\cite{zeng2024shieldgemma}, WildGuard~\cite{han2024wildguard}, Aegis~\cite{ghosh2024aegis}, Granite Guardian~\cite{padhi2024granite}). Because they ignore the specific fine-tuning recipe, they \textit{miss recipe-specific failure modes} (e.g., jailbreaks that align with newly learned domain syntax) and waste capacity on out-of-distribution risks the deployed model never faces.

Existing defenses thus force a choice between per-checkpoint training cost (families i and ii) and recipe-blind protection (family iii).

%\paragraph{Our research question.}
In this work, we pose and study the following question:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Is it possible to train a generalizable hypernetwork that, given any fine-tuned checkpoint, efficiently produces a lightweight side network restoring the safety behavior of the original aligned model --- without retraining, without modifying the deployed weights, and without sacrificing downstream accuracy?}
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Our motivation.} Our key insight is that the Pareto frontier between safety and utility exists \emph{only because the safety mechanism shares parameters with the task mechanism}. Delegating safety to a separate, conditionally engaged side network~\cite{sung2022lst} that runs in parallel with the frozen fine-tuned model breaks this coupling: (a)~the fine-tuned model retains its full task capacity, and (b)~the side network can be trained to restore refusal on harmful prompts. The ensemble inherits the utility of the fine-tuned model and the safety of the aligned base model --- removing the trade-off rather than balancing it.

The remaining problem is cost: training a bespoke side network for every fine-tuned checkpoint is impractical at the scale of the adapter ecosystem. We address this by training a hypernetwork~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks, chauhan2024brief} that, given a small set of activation fingerprints from a new fine-tuned model, generates the side network's weights in a single forward pass --- no gradient updates, no safety data needed at deployment time. The hypernetwork is trained once and amortizes across all subsequent fine-tuned checkpoints.

\paragraph{\method{}.} We introduce \method{}, which makes this generation tractable through three design choices, each addressing a specific obstacle in mapping a fine-tuned checkpoint to safety weights:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textit{What signal to condition on.} Rather than embedding text descriptions of the fine-tuning recipe (which may be unknown at deployment), we condition on \textbf{activation fingerprints} --- a per-prompt summary of how the frozen model's intermediate representations have shifted under fine-tuning, computable in a single forward pass with no gradient computation.
    \item \textit{How to make high-dimensional weight generation stable.} Each side-network layer is generated from both the corresponding base-layer activation (depth-specific specialization) and a global mean across selected base layers (cross-layer coherence). This \textbf{local~+~global conditioning} provides both depth-specific specialization and cross-layer consistency in the generated weights.
    \item \textit{How to keep inference cheap.} A $\lambda$-classifier predicts a routing probability per prompt; if $\lambda < 0.5$ the base model runs as-is, and if $\lambda \geq 0.5$ a fixed refusal template is substituted. This \textbf{hard-threshold routing} eliminates decode-time overhead on safe prompts and yields a net speed-up on harmful ones.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why this is practical.}
The side network adds only $\sim$3\% parameters over the base model, with no decode-time overhead on the typical benign-prompt path. The hypernetwork is base-model-agnostic and generalizes across fine-tuning recipes (12 unseen domains $\times$ 2 architectures), demonstrating that a single trained hypernetwork can be deployed against new checkpoints zero-shot. \paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
    \item We formulate safety restoration as \textit{activation-conditioned generation}: the side network is automatically produced from each fine-tuned model's activation fingerprint, requiring neither per-model training nor safety data at deployment.
    \item We design \method{}, combining layer-wise factorized weight generation with local+global activation conditioning to make high-dimensional weight prediction stable and generalizable.
    \item We validate on two architectures (Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B): the hypernetwork is trained on 22 fine-tuning datasets and evaluated zero-shot on unseen fine-tuned checkpoints from three holdout splits of 4 held-out datasets each (12 held-out evaluations total), using three safety benchmarks (BeaverTails plus two fully zero-shot: AdvBench and HEx-PHI). \method{} achieves near-complete harmful-prompt elimination with $<$1pp accuracy loss vs.\ the unmodified fine-tuned model.
\end{enumerate}
