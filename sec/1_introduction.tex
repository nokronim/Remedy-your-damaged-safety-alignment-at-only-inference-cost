\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Fine-tuning safety-aligned LLMs is a common way to adapt them to downstream tasks, but it can also weaken their safety alignment. Prior work has shown that fine-tuning can compromise safety even when the fine-tuning data is entirely benign~\citep{qi2024finetuning}; in our experiments, LoRA fine-tuning on benign task data raises average harmful rates from under 5\% to 19--31\% (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). One possible reason is that safety alignment can depend heavily on the model's behavior in the first few output tokens~\citep{qi2025safety}; narrow fine-tuning can even induce broadly misaligned behavior~\citep{betley2025emergent}. As a result, even benign fine-tuning updates may change the model's refusal behavior and reduce its ability to reject harmful requests.

Existing defenses fall into three families (Section~\ref{sec:related_works}; Table~\ref{tab:comparison}). \emph{Fine-tuning-stage} methods constrain task adaptation, but require control over the fine-tuning process and cannot be applied to checkpoints that have already been released. \emph{Post-fine-tuning weight-modification} methods repair a released checkpoint by projecting, pruning, or merging its weights. Some need the aligned base model at deployment, and their safety--utility balance depends on the method and its operating point. In our experiments, OneShot SFT~\citep{zhang2026safety} lowers average task accuracy by 15.3 points on LLaMA-3-8B, while Antidote~\citep{huang2025antidote} and linear merging~\citep{farn2025safeguard} maintain accuracy within approximately two points of the fine-tuned model but retain 4--9\% harmful responses on BeaverTails and HEx-PHI (Section~\ref{sec:posthoc}). \emph{Model-agnostic guard classifiers}~\citep{inan2023llama,han2024wildguard} leave the model untouched, but are not tailored to how a given checkpoint was adapted and run a separate multi-billion-parameter model on every prompt.

\noindent\begin{minipage}{\linewidth}
\setlength{\parindent}{0em}\indent Based on these limitations, we ask the following question:
\begin{quote}
\textit{Can we recover the safety behavior of the original aligned model for a given fine-tuned checkpoint using a model-specific lightweight post-hoc method, without retraining, modifying the deployed weights, or degrading downstream task performance?}
\end{quote}
\end{minipage}

Such a method should work without control over fine-tuning, leave the deployed weights unchanged, and be specific to the checkpoint. We attach a small \emph{Safe Side Network} (\ssn{}) that runs alongside the frozen model and decides whether to answer or refuse, and use a hypernetwork to generate its weights from the checkpoint's activation fingerprints, avoiding per-checkpoint \ssn{} training.

We propose \method{} (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). Given a fine-tuned checkpoint, it extracts layer-wise activation fingerprints from a small set of calibration prompts, generates the \ssn{} weights with one hypernetwork forward pass, and attaches the \ssn{} to the frozen model; prompts scored as harmful are routed to a refusal, and all others are answered by the original model. \method{} is thus checkpoint-specific, post-hoc, and non-invasive. Training the hypernetwork is a one-time cost per backbone family that requires safety data; deploying on each new checkpoint requires neither.

\mysection{Contributions.} We summarize our main contributions as follows:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=2pt]
    \item We formulate post-hoc safety restoration as the activation-conditioned generation of \ssn{}s. Given a fine-tuned checkpoint, our method generates a model-specific \ssn{} from its activation fingerprints, without additional gradient updates or safety data at deployment time.

    \item We design \method{}, a hypernetwork-based framework that predicts the weights of a lightweight \ssn{}. To make this prediction feasible, \method{} uses layer-wise activation conditioning, direction-magnitude decomposition, and factorized weight generation.

    \item We show on Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B that the generated \ssn{} restores refusal behavior on held-out fine-tuned checkpoints while preserving downstream task performance: harmful response rates fall from 19--31\% to below 1\% with less than 1\% average loss in task accuracy. It outperforms a shared \ssn{} trained on the same data, and attains lower harmful rates than weight-modification and guard-model baselines at their evaluated operating points while matching the aligned model's over-refusal.
\end{enumerate}
