\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models are typically deployed with safety alignment mechanisms that suppress harmful outputs~\cite{ouyang2022training, bai2022training}. 
However, this alignment is fragile, as downstream fine-tuning, even on benign datasets, can degrade safe behavior after relatively few training steps~\cite{qi2024finetuning, huang2024lisa, fraser2025finetuninglowers}. 
This issue is more severe in the open-weight ecosystem, where numerous community-contributed LoRA adapters and merged checkpoints are shared without guarantees that safety properties are preserved. 

Consequently, substantial research effort has been devoted to mitigating this problem. 
However, existing approaches have notable limitations. 
Methods that retrain safety mechanisms for each fine-tuned model~\cite{huang2024lisa, hsu2024safe, yi2024vaccine} incur substantial per-model cost, while model-agnostic classifiers~\cite{inan2023llama, han2024wildguard} fail to account for model-specific shifts introduced during fine-tuning. 
As a result, current solutions either scale poorly or lack adaptation to individual models.

We propose a different approach based on the hypothesis that each fine-tuning procedure induces characteristic shifts in internal representations, which can be used as a fingerprint to generate a model-specific safety classifier. 
Our key idea is to treat these representation shifts as a signature of the fine-tuned model. 
We posit that activation patterns across layers encode information about how safety-relevant features have been modified, even when fine-tuning is performed on unrelated tasks. 
Building on this observation, we train a hypernetwork~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks} to map such activation fingerprints to the parameters of a safety classifier, enabling automatic adaptation without requiring harmful data at deployment time.

Concretely, we introduce \method{}, a hypernetwork that takes intermediate activations from a fine-tuned model as input and outputs the weights of a Safe Side Network (\ssn{})~\cite{sung2022lst}, which classifies prompts as safe or harmful. 
At deployment time, the hypernetwork generates a model-specific \ssn{} from a small set of calibration prompts using a single forward pass, without gradient updates or modification of the underlying model.
Experiments across fine-tuning datasets and safety benchmarks demonstrate that \method{} reduces harmful output rates from up to $43\%$ to below $1\%$ while preserving task accuracy, and outperforms existing safety alignment approaches across all evaluated benchmarks.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce activation-conditioned safety generation, in which a safety classifier is derived directly from a fine-tuned model's activation fingerprint without requiring per-model training or safety data at deployment.
    \item We propose \method{}, which combines layer-wise factorized weight generation with direction-magnitude activation decomposition to enable efficient prediction of high-dimensional classifier parameters.
    \item We evaluate our approach on two architectures (Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B) across 22 fine-tuning datasets with multiple holdout splits, and on three safety benchmarks, demonstrating strong harmful behavior suppression with minimal impact on task performance.
\end{enumerate}

Yet this alignment is brittle: downstream fine-tuning---even on purely benign data---can erode refusal behavior in as few as a hundred steps~\cite{qi2024finetuning, huang2024lisa, fraser2025finetuninglowers}. The open-weight ecosystem amplifies the risk: thousands of community-contributed LoRA adapters and merged checkpoints circulate without any guarantee that the original safety properties survive. Existing defenses either retrain each checkpoint individually~\cite{huang2024lisa, hsu2024safe, yi2024vaccine}, incurring per-model cost, or deploy model-agnostic classifiers~\cite{inan2023llama, han2024wildguard} that cannot account for how a specific fine-tuning recipe has shifted safety boundaries.
