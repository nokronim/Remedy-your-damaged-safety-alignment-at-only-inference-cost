% ABSTRACT
% ===========================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Fine-tuning degrades the safety alignment of large language models, yet existing defenses require per-model retraining or apply model-agnostic classifiers that ignore each checkpoint's specific vulnerabilities.
We propose \method{}, a hypernetwork that generates a model-specific safety classifier at inference time from a compact activation fingerprint---no additional training, safety data, or model modification required. Layer-wise factorized weight generation and direction-magnitude activation decomposition make predicting the full safety-head parameters tractable from only 50 calibration prompts.
Evaluated on Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B across 22 fine-tuning domains and three safety benchmarks, \method{} reduces harmful output rates to near zero while preserving task accuracy, and generalizes zero-shot to AdvBench and HEx-PHI.
\end{abstract}

% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models ship with safety alignment that curbs harmful outputs~\cite{ouyang2022training, bai2022training}. Yet this alignment is brittle: downstream fine-tuning---even on purely benign data---can erode refusal behavior in as few as a hundred steps~\cite{qi2024finetuning, huang2024lisa, fraser2025finetuninglowers}. The open-weight ecosystem amplifies the risk: thousands of community-contributed LoRA adapters and merged checkpoints circulate without any guarantee that the original safety properties survive.

Current defenses fall into two camps, each with a fundamental limitation.
\textit{Post-hoc realignment}~\cite{huang2024lisa, hsu2024safe, yi2024vaccine} retrains each checkpoint individually, incurring per-model cost and requiring access to safety data.
\textit{External classifiers}~\cite{inan2023llama, han2024wildguard} operate independently of the target model, so they cannot account for how a particular fine-tuning recipe has shifted the model's safety boundary.

We propose a different approach: rather than retraining or applying a one-size-fits-all classifier, we \textit{generate} a safety mechanism tailored to each fine-tuned model automatically. Since each fine-tuning recipe shifts the model's internal representations in a distinct way, we use these representation shifts as a fingerprint to produce a model-specific safety classifier.

Concretely, we introduce \method{}, a \textit{hypernetwork}~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks} that takes intermediate activations from any fine-tuned model as input and outputs the weights of a small \textit{safety head}---a side network~\cite{sung2022lst} that classifies each prompt as safe or harmful. At deployment, a single forward pass through the hypernetwork produces a model-specific safety head from only 50 calibration prompts, with no gradient computation and no model modification.

% \remark{Figure 1: pipeline overview}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce \textbf{activation-conditioned safety generation}: the safety classifier is automatically produced from each fine-tuned model's activation fingerprint, requiring no per-model training or safety data at deployment.
    \item We propose \textbf{\method{}}, combining layer-wise factorized weight generation with direction-magnitude activation decomposition to make high-dimensional weight prediction tractable.
    \item We validate on \textbf{two architectures}, \textbf{12 unseen domains}, and \textbf{three safety benchmarks}, demonstrating near-complete harmful elimination with negligible accuracy loss.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Safety Degradation under Fine-Tuning.}
\citet{qi2024finetuning} show that fine-tuning on benign data compromises safety. \citet{qi2025tokensdeep} find alignment is shallow, encoded in only a few token positions. Defenses include LISA~\cite{huang2024lisa} (lazy alignment during training), Safe LoRA~\cite{hsu2024safe} (projection of LoRA updates), and Vaccine~\cite{yi2024vaccine} (perturbation-aware alignment). All require per-checkpoint intervention.

\paragraph{Safety Classifiers and Guardrails.}
Llama Guard~\cite{inan2023llama} and WildGuard~\cite{han2024wildguard} classify prompts independently of the target model.
HiddenDetect~\cite{jiang2025hiddendetect} monitors hidden states but requires per-model probe fitting.
These methods are either model-agnostic (ignoring fine-tuning effects) or per-model (not transferable).

\paragraph{Hypernetworks and Side Networks.}
Hypernetworks~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks, chauhan2024brief} generate target-network weights conditioned on context, with applications in continual learning~\cite{vonoswald2020continual} and multi-task adaptation~\cite{navon2023equivariant}. Recent work has scaled this paradigm to LLMs: \citet{liang2025dragdrop} generate LoRA parameters directly from task descriptions, achieving zero-shot adaptation without gradient-based fine-tuning. Our work pursues a complementary direction---rather than generating task-adaptation weights from text prompts, we generate \textit{safety} weights from model activations, targeting the orthogonal problem of safety restoration.
Ladder Side-Tuning (LST)~\cite{sung2022lst} attaches a small side network to a frozen backbone for parameter-efficient transfer. We are, to our knowledge, the first to combine hypernetworks with side-network architectures for safety restoration.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\method{} operates in three phases: (1)~collect ground-truth safety heads for a set of training domains (\S\ref{sec:gt}), (2)~train a hypernetwork to predict safety head weights from model activations (\S\ref{sec:hypernet}), and (3)~at inference, generate a safety head for any new fine-tuned model in one forward pass (\S\ref{sec:deploy}).

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathcal{M}_0$ be a safety-aligned base LLM and $\mathcal{M}_d$ a variant fine-tuned on domain $d$. Fine-tuning may cause $\mathcal{M}_d$ to comply with harmful prompts that $\mathcal{M}_0$ would refuse. We seek a generator $G$ that, given any fine-tuned $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$ (possibly from an unseen domain), produces a safety classifier $f_\theta$:
\begin{equation}
    \theta = G\!\big(\mathbf{A}(\mathcal{M}_{d^*})\big), \quad \lambda = f_\theta(x \mid \mathcal{M}_{d^*})
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{A}(\cdot)$ denotes activations from a small calibration set and $\lambda \in [0,1]$ is a safety score ($\lambda \approx 0$: safe; $\lambda \approx 1$: harmful).

\subsection{Safety Head Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

We build on the Ladder Side-Tuning (LST) architecture~\cite{sung2022lst} and extend it for safety classification.

\paragraph{Side Transformer.} $K{=}12$ layers with hidden dimension $h_s = h/r$ ($r{=}4$). For Qwen2-7B: $h_s{=}896$, 7 heads; for LLaMA-3-8B: $h_s{=}1024$, 8 heads ($\sim$237M parameters, $\sim$3\% of the backbone).

\paragraph{Ladder Connections.} Each side layer $k$ fuses downsampled backbone hidden states with side hidden states via a learnable gate:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}^{(k)}_\text{in} = \mu_k \cdot W_\text{down}\, \mathbf{h}^{(l)}_\text{back} + (1{-}\mu_k) \cdot \mathbf{h}^{(k-1)}_\text{side}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_k = \sigma(\alpha_k/\tau)$ is a learned gate.

\paragraph{Selective Routing.} After the final side layer, hidden states are mean-pooled and passed through an MLP to produce $\lambda$. At inference, prompts scoring above the threshold are routed to a refusal response; those below bypass the side network entirely, so the base model runs unmodified. This selective routing is what preserves task accuracy: safe prompts never interact with the safety head.

\subsection{Ground-Truth Collection}
\label{sec:gt}

We collect supervised pairs $\{(\mathcal{M}_d, \theta_d^*)\}_{d=1}^K$ by training one side network per domain:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fine-tune $\mathcal{M}_0$ on domain $d$ with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$); merge the adapter to obtain $\mathcal{M}_d$.
    \item Freeze $\mathcal{M}_d$; attach a randomly initialized side network.
    \item Train the side network on a mixture of domain-specific safe data and category-balanced harmful data from BeaverTails~\cite{ji2024beavertails} ($\sim$7k samples).
\end{enumerate}

Training uses a \textbf{selective loss}: harmful prompts receive both language-modeling loss and classification loss; safe prompts receive only classification loss. This teaches the side network \textit{when} to intervene without degrading task performance on safe inputs. All side networks share the same initialization, ensuring weight-space alignment for hypernetwork supervision.

\subsection{Hypernetwork}
\label{sec:hypernet}

The hypernetwork $G$ maps an activation fingerprint to $\sim$237M side-network parameters. We make this tractable through two complementary strategies.

\subsubsection{Activation Extraction}

For each model $\mathcal{M}_d$, we run $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ domain-specific calibration prompts and average the hidden state at each layer $l$ after the final token:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a}_l^{(d)} = \frac{1}{N_\text{cal}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{cal}} \mathbf{h}_l^{(d)}(x_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{h}
\end{equation}
These $L$ vectors form a compact behavioral fingerprint of how fine-tuning shifted the model's representations.

\subsubsection{Layer-wise Factorized Generation}

Rather than predicting all parameters jointly, we generate each side layer's weights independently from the corresponding backbone activation:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k = H_k(\mathbf{a}_{l(k)})
\end{equation}
The modules $H_k$ share most parameters across layers but include layer-specific adapters, reducing the effective output dimensionality per prediction.

\subsubsection{Direction-Magnitude Decomposition}

Each activation vector is split into a unit direction and log-magnitude:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{d}}_l = \frac{\mathbf{a}_l}{\|\mathbf{a}_l\|}, \quad m_l = \log \|\mathbf{a}_l\|
\end{equation}
The direction encodes \textit{what} changed; the magnitude encodes \textit{how much}. Separate encoders process each component:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_\text{dir} = \text{Attn}(W_\text{proj}\,\hat{\mathbf{d}}_l), \quad
    \mathbf{z}_\text{mag} = \text{MLP}(m_l)
\end{equation}
The fused representation $[\mathbf{z}_\text{dir};\, \mathbf{z}_\text{mag}]$ feeds the weight-generation MLP.

\subsubsection{Classifier Generation}

The safety classifier is generated via a dedicated branch that aggregates information across all layers using cross-layer attention, producing a global embedding of the model's behavioral shift. This embedding is mapped through an MLP with a low-rank output factorization ($W_\text{cls} = B \cdot A$, rank $r{=}16$) for efficiency.

\subsubsection{Training Losses}

The hypernetwork is trained on $K$ domains (with $M$ held out) using three complementary losses:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{recon} + \alpha\, \mathcal{L}_\text{cls} + \beta\, \mathcal{L}_\text{func}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{recon}$ is the MSE between generated and ground-truth side network weights, providing dense supervision across all parameters; $\mathcal{L}_\text{cls}$ is BCE on safety score predictions using the calibration prompts ($\alpha{=}10$); and $\mathcal{L}_\text{func}$ is BCE on a separate batch of safe and harmful probe prompts ($\beta{=}5$), verifying that the generated classifier generalizes beyond the calibration set.

Training runs for 100 epochs with learning rate $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and hidden dimension 512.

\subsection{Deployment}
\label{sec:deploy}

Given a new fine-tuned model $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$: (1)~extract layer-wise activations from $N_\text{cal}$ calibration prompts; (2)~run one forward pass through the hypernetwork to obtain safety head weights; (3)~load the safety head alongside $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$. The entire process completes in under one minute on a single GPU, with no safety data, no gradients, and no model modification.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Models and Domains.} We evaluate on Qwen2-7B-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen2} and LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct~\cite{dubey2024llama} across 22 fine-tuning domains spanning QA, commonsense reasoning, math, knowledge, classification, code, and instruction-following (Appendix~\ref{app:domains}). Each domain is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$) and merged.

\paragraph{Safety Benchmarks.} We evaluate on three harmful prompt benchmarks: BeaverTails~\cite{ji2024beavertails} (700 prompts, 14 categories), AdvBench~\cite{zou2023universal} (520 prompts), and HEx-PHI~\cite{qi2024hexphi} (300 prompts, 11 categories). BeaverTails is used as the harmful data source during \textit{side network training} (\S\ref{sec:gt}); the evaluation split is disjoint from training. AdvBench and HEx-PHI are never seen in any training phase.

\paragraph{Metrics.} \textbf{Task Accuracy}: domain test performance. \textbf{Harmful Rate}: percentage of harmful prompts producing harmful output (lower is safer), judged by the beaver-dam-7b moderation model.

\paragraph{Evaluation Protocol.} Three holdout splits (Table~\ref{tab:splits}), each removing four domains from different categories. The hypernetwork trains on the remaining 18 domains.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}
\label{sec:ablations}

We ablate on LLaMA-3-8B, reporting task accuracy change and harmful rate (BeaverTails). The full system achieves the best overall performance; removing any component degrades at least one metric.

\paragraph{Loss Components (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_loss}).}
Removing $\mathcal{L}_\text{func}^\text{safe}$ (safe-probe supervision) causes the largest accuracy drop: without learning what ``safe'' looks like, the classifier over-blocks legitimate prompts. Removing all functional supervision ($-\mathcal{L}_\text{func}$) hurts both accuracy and safety---the weight-only MSE signal cannot ensure behavioral correctness. Removing domain-specific calibration prompts has the largest safety impact (harmful rate rises from 0.1\% to 4.7\%), as the activation fingerprint becomes less domain-discriminative. Each component addresses a distinct failure mode.

\subsection{Cross-Benchmark Safety Transfer}

BeaverTails is the only harmful benchmark used during side network training (with a disjoint evaluation split). To test whether generated safety heads generalize beyond BeaverTails, we evaluate on AdvBench (520 prompts) and HEx-PHI (300 prompts, 11 categories), which are never seen in any training phase. Across both models and all holdout domains, the harmful rate remains at 0\% on AdvBench and below 1.5\% on the more challenging HEx-PHI (full per-domain breakdown in Appendix~\ref{app:per_domain}). This confirms that \method{} captures general harmful-content patterns rather than benchmark-specific features.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\method{} generates model-specific safety classifiers at inference time by mapping fine-tuned model activations to safety head weights through a hypernetwork. It virtually eliminates harmful outputs across diverse domains and two model architectures while preserving task accuracy, and transfers zero-shot to unseen safety benchmarks. Ablations reveal that behavioral probe supervision and sufficient training diversity are the most critical ingredients. The approach offers a scalable path toward safety restoration for the growing ecosystem of fine-tuned language models.

\paragraph{Future Work.} Generation-level safety monitoring, scaling to 70B+ models, cross-architecture transfer, and robustness to adversarial jailbreak attacks.


\section*{Limitations}

Our approach requires a one-time setup cost: ground-truth side networks must be trained for each of the training domains before hypernetwork training can begin. The safety classifier operates at the prompt level and does not monitor generated outputs, so harmful completions of seemingly benign prompts may not be caught. We evaluate on 7--8B parameter models; scaling behavior to significantly larger models remains an open question. Finally, while we test on three safety benchmarks, evaluation on adversarial jailbreak attacks and multi-turn conversations would further validate robustness.

\section{Implementation Details}
\label{app:implementation}

\paragraph{Side Network.} Qwen2-7B: $h_s{=}896$, 7 heads, 12 layers ($\sim$237M, 2.88\% of backbone). LLaMA-3-8B: $h_s{=}1024$, 8 heads, 12 layers.

\paragraph{LoRA Fine-Tuning.} Rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$, all linear layers, 3 epochs, lr $2{\times}10^{-4}$.

\paragraph{Hypernetwork.} Hidden dim 512, classifier LoRA rank 16, lr $5{\times}10^{-5}$, 100 epochs, $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ prompts, direction projection dim 256, magnitude hidden dim 128. Loss weights: $\alpha{=}10$, $\beta{=}5$.
