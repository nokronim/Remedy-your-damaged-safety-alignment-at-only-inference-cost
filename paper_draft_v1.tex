% ABSTRACT
% ===========================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Safety alignment in large language models is fragile: even benign fine-tuning can degrade refusal behavior without any malicious intent. Existing mitigations either require per-model retraining or ignore how fine-tuning reshapes a model's safety boundaries.
We propose \method{}, a hypernetwork that generates a lightweight, model-specific safety classifier at inference time---no additional training required. Given a fine-tuned checkpoint, \method{} extracts a compact activation fingerprint from a small calibration set and maps it, in a single forward pass, to the weights of a side network that acts as a prompt-level safety head. Two key design choices make the high-dimensional weight generation tractable: layer-wise factorization and direction-magnitude decomposition of activations.
Evaluated on Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B across 22 fine-tuning domains (three holdout splits, 12 unseen domains), \method{} reduces harmful output rates from up to 43\% to below 0.5\% while preserving task accuracy within 1\%. Safety transfers zero-shot to AdvBench (100\%) and HEx-PHI (97--100\%), benchmarks never seen during training.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Large language models ship with safety alignment that curbs harmful outputs~\cite{ouyang2022training, bai2022training}. Yet this alignment is brittle: downstream fine-tuning---even on purely benign data---can erode refusal behavior in as few as a hundred steps~\cite{qi2024finetuning, huang2024lisa, fraser2025finetuninglowers}. The open-weight ecosystem amplifies the risk: thousands of community-contributed LoRA adapters and merged checkpoints circulate without any guarantee that the original safety properties survive.

Current defenses fall into two camps, each with a fundamental limitation.
\textit{Post-hoc realignment}~\cite{huang2024lisa, hsu2024safe, yi2024vaccine} retrains each checkpoint individually, incurring per-model cost and requiring access to safety data.
\textit{External classifiers}~\cite{inan2023llama, han2024wildguard} operate independently of the target model, so they cannot account for how a particular fine-tuning recipe has shifted the model's safety boundary.

We start from a different premise: \textbf{prompt safety is model-dependent}. The same prompt may be refused by one fine-tuned variant and complied with by another, depending on the domain and data mixture. An effective safety mechanism must therefore be \textit{conditioned on the specific model}.

\method{} operationalizes this insight (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}). We train a \textit{hypernetwork}~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks} that takes intermediate activations from any fine-tuned model as input and outputs the weights of a small \textit{safety head}---a side network~\cite{sung2022lst} that classifies each prompt as safe or harmful. At deployment, a single forward pass through the hypernetwork produces a model-specific safety head from only 50 calibration prompts, with no gradient computation and no model modification.

% \remark{Figure 1: pipeline overview}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce \textbf{model-conditioned safety assessment}: the safety classifier is generated as a function of each fine-tuned model's activation fingerprint, rather than being shared or retrained per model.
    \item We propose \textbf{\method{}}, combining layer-wise factorized weight generation with direction-magnitude activation decomposition to make 237M-parameter weight prediction tractable from a handful of activation vectors.
    \item We validate on \textbf{two architectures} (Qwen2-7B, LLaMA-3-8B), \textbf{12 unseen holdout domains}, and \textbf{three safety benchmarks}, showing $>$99\% harmful detection with $<$1\% accuracy drop.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Safety Degradation under Fine-Tuning.}
\citet{qi2024finetuning} show that fine-tuning on benign data compromises safety. \citet{qi2025tokensdeep} find alignment is shallow, encoded in only a few token positions. Defenses include LISA~\cite{huang2024lisa} (lazy alignment during training), Safe LoRA~\cite{hsu2024safe} (projection of LoRA updates), and Vaccine~\cite{yi2024vaccine} (perturbation-aware alignment). All require per-checkpoint intervention.

\paragraph{Safety Classifiers and Guardrails.}
Llama Guard~\cite{inan2023llama} and WildGuard~\cite{han2024wildguard} classify prompts independently of the target model.
HiddenDetect~\cite{jiang2025hiddendetect} monitors hidden states but requires per-model probe fitting.
These methods are either model-agnostic (ignoring fine-tuning effects) or per-model (not transferable).

\paragraph{Hypernetworks and Side Networks.}
Hypernetworks~\cite{ha2017hypernetworks, chauhan2024brief} generate target-network weights conditioned on context, with applications in continual learning~\cite{vonoswald2020continual} and multi-task adaptation~\cite{navon2023equivariant}.
Ladder Side-Tuning (LST)~\cite{sung2022lst} attaches a small side network to a frozen backbone for parameter-efficient transfer. We are, to our knowledge, the first to combine hypernetworks with side-network architectures for safety restoration.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\method{} operates in three phases: (1) collect ground-truth safety heads for a set of training domains (\S\ref{sec:gt}), (2) train a hypernetwork to predict safety head weights from model activations (\S\ref{sec:hypernet}), and (3) at inference, generate a safety head for any new fine-tuned model in one forward pass (\S\ref{sec:deploy}).

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

Let $\mathcal{M}_0$ be a safety-aligned base LLM and $\mathcal{M}_d$ a variant fine-tuned on domain $d$. Fine-tuning may cause $\mathcal{M}_d$ to comply with harmful prompts that $\mathcal{M}_0$ would refuse. We seek a generator $G$ that, given any fine-tuned $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$ (possibly from an unseen domain), produces a safety classifier $f_\theta$:
\begin{equation}
    \theta = G\!\big(\mathbf{A}(\mathcal{M}_{d^*})\big), \quad \lambda = f_\theta(x \mid \mathcal{M}_{d^*})
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{A}(\cdot)$ denotes activations from a small calibration set and $\lambda \in [0,1]$ is a safety score ($\lambda \approx 0$: safe; $\lambda \approx 1$: harmful).

\subsection{Safety Head Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}

The safety head is a side transformer~\cite{sung2022lst} with three extensions for safety classification.

\paragraph{Side Transformer.} $K{=}12$ layers with hidden dimension $h_s = h/r$ ($r{=}4$). For Qwen2-7B: $h_s{=}896$, 7 heads; for LLaMA-3-8B: $h_s{=}1024$, 8 heads ($\sim$237M parameters, $\sim$3\% of the backbone).

\paragraph{Ladder Connections.} Each side layer $k$ fuses downsampled backbone hidden states with side hidden states via a learnable gate:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}^{(k)}_\text{in} = \mu_k \cdot W_\text{down}\, \mathbf{h}^{(l)}_\text{back} + (1{-}\mu_k) \cdot \mathbf{h}^{(k-1)}_\text{side}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_k = \sigma(\alpha_k/\tau)$ is a learned gate.

\paragraph{Lambda Classifier and Selective Routing.} After the final side layer, hidden states are mean-pooled and passed through an MLP to produce $\lambda$. At inference, prompts with $\lambda > 0.5$ are routed to a refusal response; prompts with $\lambda \leq 0.5$ bypass the side network entirely, so the base model runs unmodified. This selective routing is what preserves task accuracy: safe prompts never interact with the safety head.

\subsection{Ground-Truth Collection}
\label{sec:gt}

We collect supervised pairs $\{(\mathcal{M}_d, \theta_d^*)\}_{d=1}^K$ by training one side network per domain:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fine-tune $\mathcal{M}_0$ on domain $d$ with LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$); merge the adapter to obtain $\mathcal{M}_d$.
    \item Freeze $\mathcal{M}_d$; attach a randomly initialized side network.
    \item Train the side network on a mixture of domain-specific safe data and category-balanced harmful data from BeaverTails~\cite{ji2024beavertails} ($\sim$7k samples).
\end{enumerate}

Training uses a \textbf{selective loss}: harmful prompts receive both language-modeling loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{SFT}$) and classification loss ($\mathcal{L}_\text{cls}$); safe prompts receive only $\mathcal{L}_\text{cls}$. This teaches the side network \textit{when} to intervene without degrading task performance on safe inputs.

All 22 side networks share the same initialization and training procedure, so their weight spaces are naturally aligned---no permutation ambiguity arises. This makes direct weight-space supervision well-defined.

\subsection{Hypernetwork}
\label{sec:hypernet}

The hypernetwork $G$ maps an activation fingerprint to $\sim$237M side-network parameters. We make this tractable through two complementary strategies.

\subsubsection{Activation Extraction}

For each model $\mathcal{M}_d$, we run $N_\text{cal}{=}50$ domain-specific calibration prompts and average the hidden state at each layer $l$ after the final token:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a}_l^{(d)} = \frac{1}{N_\text{cal}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_\text{cal}} \mathbf{h}_l^{(d)}(x_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{h}
\end{equation}
These $L$ vectors form a compact behavioral fingerprint of how fine-tuning shifted the model's representations.

\subsubsection{Layer-wise Factorized Generation}

Rather than predicting all parameters jointly, we generate each side layer's weights independently from the corresponding backbone activation:
\begin{equation}
    \theta_k = H_k(\mathbf{a}_{l(k)})
\end{equation}
The modules $H_k$ share most parameters across layers but include layer-specific adapters, reducing the effective output dimensionality per prediction.

\subsubsection{Direction-Magnitude Decomposition}

Each activation vector is split into a unit direction and log-magnitude:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{d}}_l = \frac{\mathbf{a}_l}{\|\mathbf{a}_l\|}, \quad m_l = \log \|\mathbf{a}_l\|
\end{equation}
The direction encodes \textit{what} changed; the magnitude encodes \textit{how much}. Separate encoders process each component:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_\text{dir} = \text{Attn}(W_\text{proj}\,\hat{\mathbf{d}}_l), \quad
    \mathbf{z}_\text{mag} = \text{MLP}(m_l)
\end{equation}
The fused representation $[\mathbf{z}_\text{dir};\, \mathbf{z}_\text{mag}]$ feeds the weight-generation MLP.

\subsubsection{Lambda Classifier Generation}

The safety classifier is generated via a dedicated branch that first aggregates information across all layers using cross-layer attention, producing a global embedding of the model's behavioral shift. This embedding is mapped through an MLP with a low-rank output factorization ($W_\text{cls} = B \cdot A$, rank $r{=}16$) for efficiency.

\subsubsection{Training Losses}

The hypernetwork is trained on $K$ domains (with $M$ held out) using three complementary losses:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{recon}}_\text{weight MSE}
              + \alpha \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{cls}}_\text{calibration BCE}
              + \beta  \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_\text{func}}_\text{probe BCE}
\end{equation}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
    \item $\mathcal{L}_\text{recon}$: MSE between generated and ground-truth weights, providing dense supervision across all $\sim$237M dimensions.
    \item $\mathcal{L}_\text{cls}$: BCE on $\lambda$ predictions using the same calibration prompts used for activation extraction ($\alpha{=}10$).
    \item $\mathcal{L}_\text{func}$: BCE on a \textit{separate} set of held-out safe and harmful probe prompts ($\beta{=}5$), ensuring generalization beyond calibration data. We show in \S\ref{sec:ablations} that removing the safe-probe component of this loss causes complete classifier collapse.
\end{itemize}

Training runs for 100 epochs with learning rate $5 \times 10^{-5}$ and hidden dimension 512.

\subsection{Deployment}
\label{sec:deploy}

Given a new fine-tuned model $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$: (1)~extract layer-wise activations from $N_\text{cal}$ calibration prompts; (2)~run one forward pass through the hypernetwork to obtain safety head weights; (3)~load the safety head alongside $\mathcal{M}_{d^*}$. For each input prompt, the safety head computes $\lambda$ and routes accordingly. The entire process completes in under one minute on a single GPU, with no safety data, no gradients, and no model modification.

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Models and Domains.} We evaluate on Qwen2-7B-Instruct~\cite{yang2024qwen2} and LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct~\cite{dubey2024llama}. We construct 22 fine-tuning domains spanning QA (BoolQ, NQ-Open, SQuAD, ARC, PubMedQA), commonsense (CommonsenseQA, PIQA, HellaSwag, SocialIQA), math (GSM8K, MATH), knowledge (MMLU, Platypus), classification (AG News), code (CodeFeedback), and instruction-following (Dolly, OpenOrca, Alpaca, FLAN, OpenHermes, NoRobots, WizardLM). Each domain is fine-tuned with LoRA (rank 16, $\alpha{=}32$) and merged. Full details in Appendix~\ref{app:domains}.

\paragraph{Safety Benchmarks.} BeaverTails~\cite{ji2024beavertails} (700 prompts, 14 categories)---used during training; AdvBench~\cite{zou2023universal} (520 prompts) and HEx-PHI~\cite{qi2024hexphi} (300 prompts, 11 categories)---zero-shot transfer only.

\paragraph{Metrics.} \textbf{Task Accuracy}: domain test performance. \textbf{Harmful Rate}: percentage of harmful prompts that produce harmful output (lower is safer), judged by the beaver-dam-7b moderation model for BeaverTails and by keyword-based refusal detection for AdvBench and HEx-PHI.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\method{} generates model-specific safety classifiers at inference time by mapping fine-tuned model activations to safety head weights through a hypernetwork. Across two architectures, 12 unseen domains, and three safety benchmarks, it reduces harmful output rates from up to 43\% to below 0.5\% while preserving task accuracy within 1\%. Safety transfers zero-shot to AdvBench and HEx-PHI, demonstrating that the hypernetwork learns general safety patterns. Our ablations identify instruction-following training data and safe-probe behavioral supervision as the most critical ingredients. The approach offers a scalable path toward safety-as-a-service for the ecosystem of fine-tuned language models.

\paragraph{Future Work.} Generation-level safety monitoring, scaling to 70B+ models, cross-architecture transfer, and robustness to adversarial jailbreak attacks.


\section*{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Setup cost:} Ground-truth side networks must be trained for multiple domains (one-time).
    \item \textbf{Prompt-level only:} The classifier does not monitor generated outputs; harmful continuations of safe-seeming prompts may be missed.
    \item \textbf{Scale:} Evaluated on 7--8B models; behavior at 70B+ is untested.
    \item \textbf{Overhead:} The side network adds $\sim$3\% parameters and proportional inference latency.
    \item \textbf{Evaluation scope:} We do not test adversarial jailbreaks, multi-turn conversations, or multilingual settings.
\end{itemize}
